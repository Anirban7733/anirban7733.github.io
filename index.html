Publications and preprints

  1. Weighted Conformal Clustering
    With Y. Hur and G. Allen
    NeurIPS 2026 arXiv

    Clustering sorts data into groups but usually says nothing about how certain each assignment is. This paper produces, for each data point, a short list of groups it plausibly belongs to, with a guarantee, and corrects for the fact that the group labels are themselves estimated, which keeps these lists small even for complex, high-dimensional data.

  2. Inference for Clustering: Conformal Sets for Cluster Labels
    With Y. Hur and G. Allen
    Submitted to the Journal of the American Statistical Association arXiv

    When data are divided into clusters, we rarely know how much to trust each assignment. This paper gives a method that returns a set of likely clusters for each data point, guaranteed to contain the right one with a chosen probability, and applies it to identifying cell types from single-cell gene data.

  3. Linear Regression Beyond Covariance: Latent Slope Spectra from Higher-Order Cumulants
    With M. Yuan
    Submitted to Biometrika arXiv

    Standard regression reports one average effect of a variable, which can hide that different hidden parts of that variable act in different ways. This paper shows how patterns in the data beyond averages and correlations can reveal, and sometimes separate, these distinct effects, and spells out what can and cannot be concluded from observational data alone.

  4. Concentration of Adjacency and Laplacian Matrices for Lazy Network-Valued Stochastic Processes
    With S. Chatterjee et al.
    Preprint arXiv

    Real networks change over time, with individual connections switching on and off at their own pace rather than all at once. This paper shows that, even so, the network's overall structure over time is reliably close to what we would expect, which justifies standard methods for finding communities in the network and for detecting when its structure changes.

  5. Differential Fault Analysis of NORX Using Variants of the Coupon Collector Problem
    With A. Jana et al.
    Journal of Cryptographic Engineering, 2022 journal

    NORX is an encryption scheme that was thought to resist attacks based on deliberately causing hardware errors. This paper gives the first such attack on it, showing that carefully induced errors can reveal the secret key, and uses a new variant of the classic "coupon collector" probability problem to count how many errors are needed.
